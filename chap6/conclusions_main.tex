\chapter{Conclusions}

This study set out to address the critical operational gap in municipal solid waste management: the disconnect between static, ``one-size-fits-all'' policy frameworks and the non-linear behavioral reality of resource-constrained environments. By departing from traditional administrative heuristics and developing a \textit{Heuristic-Guided Deep Reinforcement Learning (HuDRL)} framework, this research successfully modeled the complex interplay between municipal budgeting, social norms, and enforcement intensity in the Municipality of Bacolod.

The simulation results provided a definitive computational answer to the ``dilution problem'' inherent in local governance. The study concluded that the administrative standard of ``Equal Distribution'' is mathematically destined to fail in high-density areas, validating the \textit{Urban Resource Trap} hypothesis where density and anonymity dissolve the social pressure that sustains compliance in rural communities \citep{Villanueva2021}. The HuDRL agent's independent discovery of the \textit{Sequential Saturation} strategy—which prioritizes the concentrated application of resources to trigger behavioral tipping points—offered a data-driven refutation of the status quo, proving that effective governance requires the political will to prioritize sequential efficacy over simultaneous inefficiency.

\section{Achieved Objectives}

The study successfully met its three primary research objectives, yielding the following critical insights:

First, the development of the behavioral-computational environment validated the \textit{Convenience Hypothesis}. The Global Sensitivity Analysis revealed that the \textit{Cost of Effort} parameter ($c_{\text{effort}}$) accounted for approximately 83\% of the variance in compliance rates. This computationally proved that structural friction—not a lack of awareness—is the primary inhibitor of waste segregation in the Philippine context \citep{Yazawa2025}. Conversely, the \textit{Attitude} parameter exhibited near-zero sensitivity, quantifying the \textit{Value-Action Gap} and confirming that Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns exhibit diminishing returns when extrinsic barriers remain high \citep{Zhao2022}.

Second, the study identified the mechanism of policy failure in the current administration. The comparative analysis demonstrated that the ``Status Quo'' strategy acts as a \textit{Dilution Mechanism}. By spreading limited funds equally across all seven barangays, the Local Government Unit (LGU) failed to generate the enforcement intensity required to alter the utility calculus of non-compliant households in high-density zones like Barangay Poblacion.

Third, the HuDRL agent successfully discovered the optimal dynamic strategy. By abandoning the heuristic of fairness in favor of \textit{Sequential Saturation}, the agent achieved a 92.8\% global compliance rate. The AI demonstrated that the optimal path to sustainability is to target specific barangays until they reach a \textit{Social Tipping Point} (approximately 70\%), thereby triggering the \textit{Social Norm Shield} that sustains behavior without perpetual funding \citep{Andre2021}.

\section{Critique and Limitations}

While the HuDRL framework provides robust strategic insights, the model is subject to specific constraints that define the scope of its applicability.

A primary limitation is the \textit{spatial abstraction} of the environment. The current model utilizes a network-based topology to represent barangays rather than continuous GIS (Geographic Information System) coordinates. Consequently, the calculation of \textit{Cost of Effort} assumes a uniform distance to collection points within a purok, averaging out micro-spatial factors such as road quality or elevation that influence real-world compliance.

Furthermore, the model assumes a \textit{homogeneity of agents}. Although agents possess distinct behavioral weights, they all belong to a single class: ``Residential Households.'' The simulation does not currently account for commercial entities (e.g., public markets) or institutional generators (e.g., schools), which likely operate under different utility functions and may respond differently to enforcement compared to residential units.

Additionally, the \textit{Social Norm Shield} was implemented as a heuristic threshold (locking $w_{sn}$ values) rather than an emergent cognitive property. While this aligns with sociological observations of habit formation \citep{Corcoran2020}, it is a simplified representation of the complex psychological process of internalization.

\section{Future Work}

To further refine the computational governance framework, future research should focus on the integration of \textit{GIS and Route Optimization}. By coupling the HuDRL agent with a Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) solver using real-world shapefiles of Bacolod, the model could simultaneously optimize budget allocation and garbage truck routing, providing a more granular calculation of the logistical costs \citep{Torkayesh2021}.

Future iterations should also introduce \textit{heterogeneous agent architectures}. Expanding the population to include distinct classes for commercial and industrial actors would allow the DRL agent to learn differential enforcement strategies—for example, applying stricter penalties to businesses while subsidizing households.

Finally, the most critical next step is the \textit{empirical pilot testing} of the \textit{Sequential Saturation} strategy. A controlled study in a single barangay should be conducted to test the ``Shock and Awe'' hypothesis: concentrating 90\% of available enforcement resources in one zone to observe if the predicted ``Tipping Point'' manifests in reality \citep{Centola2018}.

\section{Final Remarks}

This thesis demonstrates that the challenge of Solid Waste Management is not merely a logistical engineering problem, but a complex socio-technical one. The HuDRL agent did not ``solve'' waste management by magic; it solved it by revealing that the administrative intuition for ``fairness'' was mathematically inefficient in a resource-constrained system.

By proving that the binding constraint was not the total amount of money, but the strategy by which it was deployed, this study offers the Municipality of Bacolod a scientifically grounded path forward. It signifies a shift from intuition-based governance to evidence-based policy design, ensuring that municipal resources are invested where they trigger the self-sustaining social norms necessary for long-term environmental resilience.
