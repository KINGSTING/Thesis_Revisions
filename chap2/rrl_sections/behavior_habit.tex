
Designing effective and cost-efficient waste segregation policies requires a computational model grounded in established psychological theory. The Agent-Based Model (ABM) developed for this study extends the standard cognitive frameworks by integrating contemporary research on physical friction, social tipping points, and habit formation. This section outlines the psychological foundations of the agent architecture and how these behavioral constructs dictate compliance outcomes.

\subsection{The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) in Waste Management}

The foundational decision-making architecture of each household agent is built upon the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), recognized as the dominant psychological framework for explaining pro-environmental behaviors. Drawing from the study of \citet{Ceschi2021}, this theory posits that an individual's intention to perform a behavior—such as source segregation—is determined by three core cognitive constructs: \textit{Attitude} (internal valuation of the behavior), \textit{Subjective Norms} (perceived social pressure), and \textit{Perceived Behavioral Control} (the perceived ease or difficulty of executing the task).

Recent literature strongly supports the validity of utilizing TPB as the computational core of agent decision-making. A systematic review by \citet{Taraghi2025} found that ABM researchers frequently employ internal and external control variables to operationalize TPB concepts. To mathematically represent this decision-making process, the model employs a linear utility function where the agent's utility to segregate ($U_{\mathrm{segregate}}$) is defined as the sum of these weighted psychological factors:

\begin{equation}
    U_{\mathrm{segregate}} = (w_A A + w_{SN} SN + w_{PBC} PBC) - C_{\mathrm{Net}} + \epsilon
    \label{eq:behavior_equation}
\end{equation}

In this formulation, the coefficients ($w$) represent the relative weight the agent assigns to each psychological factor. Crucially, the $\epsilon$ (epsilon) term accounts for the inherent stochasticity present in human decision-making \citep{Chen2023, Zheng2022}. Because individuals do not act with perfect deterministic rationality, $\epsilon$ represents ``noise''—such as haste, forgetfulness, or daily unpredictability. Embracing this randomness is essential to produce valid insights, as real-world Solid Waste Management (SWM) systems are characterized by deep behavioral uncertainty \citep{Meng2018}.

\subsection{The Convenience Barrier (Cost of Effort)}

While internal motivations like Attitude ($A$) are frequently targeted by municipal Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns, literature increasingly demonstrates that positive intentions are easily overridden by logistical friction. This phenomenon is characterized as the ``Convenience Barrier'' or the physical ``Cost of Effort'' ($C_{\mathrm{Effort}}$). 

Studies evaluating household interventions consistently find that the physical hassle associated with washing, sorting, storing, and disposing of waste acts as a primary deterrent to compliance, heavily suppressing an individual's Perceived Behavioral Control \citep{Trushna2024, Moeini2023}. \citet{LoanBalanay2023} highlighted that without structural ``nudges'' and convenient infrastructure, even highly environmentally conscious households will abandon segregation practices. \citet{Meng2018} operationalized this in computational models by demonstrating that when the effort required to recycle exceeds a specific threshold, it entirely nullifies the positive effects of internal attitude and external incentives.

This literature directly informs the inclusion of the Net Cost ($C_{\mathrm{Net}}$) variable in the agent's utility function. It provides the theoretical foundation for the simulation's Global Sensitivity Analysis (GSA), anticipating that variations in physical friction—rather than mere awareness or attitude—will serve as the dominant driver of systemic non-compliance. Therefore, municipal policies relying solely on IEC campaigns face severe diminishing returns if the underlying logistical effort remains unaddressed \citep{Purisima2025}.

\subsection{Social Tipping Points and Cultural Inertia}

While TPB and effort-cost mechanisms effectively model the \textit{initiation} of compliance at the individual level, achieving municipal sustainability requires understanding how behaviors lock-in collectively. Contemporary literature suggests that social change is non-linear; it is driven by ``critical mass'' dynamics rather than gradual linear growth \citep{Nyborg2024}.

Experimental studies by \citet{Centola2018} demonstrated that overturning established conventions does not require majority consensus. Instead, once a committed minority reaches a ``tipping point''—historically approximated at 25\% of the population—they trigger a rapid cascade of adoption across the remaining majority. In the context of waste management, \citet{Constantino2022} define this through ``behavioral hysteresis'' and normative shields. They argue that in the absence of constant financial incentives or strict enforcement, social norms function as a substitute governance mechanism. 

To computationally capture this, the simulation incorporates a two-stage norm internalization mechanism. The first is the \textit{Bayanihan Activation} threshold (at 40\% local compliance), representing an emergent community spirit that slows the erosion of positive habits \citep{Nishimura2022}. The second is the \textit{Norm Lock-in} tipping point (at 70\% compliance). As \citet{Corcoran2020} established, once a practice crosses this upper threshold, it transitions from a calculated, incentive-driven decision into an internalized ``cognitive script'' or descriptive norm. At this stage, the social cost of deviating (peer pressure or shame) exceeds the physical cost of segregating. 

This theoretical framework is critical for optimizing resource-constrained local governance. It computationally implies that the Mayor Agent's policy interventions need not fund punitive enforcement or incentives across 100\% of the municipality indefinitely. Instead, the AI can theoretically discover strategies that sequentially concentrate resources to force individual barangays past the 70\% norm lock-in threshold, transitioning the system from a state of costly, forced compliance into a self-sustaining cultural norm \citep{Farrow2020}.
