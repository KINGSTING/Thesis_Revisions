\chapter{Proposed Smart Governance Framework}
This chapter synthesizes the computational findings into actionable governance strategies for the Municipality of Bacolod. By bridging the gap between Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) outputs and socioeconomic realities, the evaluation uncovers the specific mechanisms that dictate waste management success in the Philippine local government context \citep{Jimenez2025}.

\section{Translating Algorithm to Policy (The LGU Playbook)}
The traditional administrative heuristic of "Equal Distribution"—spreading funds evenly across all barangays—has been mathematically proven by the simulation to act as a mechanism of dilution \citep{Alphonso2024}. By distributing a limited budget equally, the Local Government Unit (LGU) ensures that the critical density of enforcement or incentives is never met in densely populated urban centers like Barangay Poblacion \citep{Villanueva2021}.

To bridge the gap between algorithmic logic and legislative reality, the LGU must formally adopt the AI's "Sequential Saturation" strategy \citep{Zheng2022b}.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phased Rollout Justification:} The Mayor and the Municipal Environment and Natural Resources Office (MENRO) must legally and politically reframe unequal budget distribution as a "Phased Rollout." The LGU should justify concentrating the overwhelming majority of its quarterly MOOE budget into Poblacion during Quarter 1, treating it as a "Pilot Zone" to break initial behavioral inertia before moving to rural areas like Demologan.
    \item \textbf{Legislative Action:} This requires drafting a Sangguniang Bayan (SB) Resolution to legally ring-fence waste management funds for targeted geographic saturation, explicitly abandoning simultaneous equality in favor of sequential efficacy \citep{Nishimura2022}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mitigating the "Cost of Effort" (Infrastructure over Information)}
The Global Sensitivity Analysis confirmed that the "Cost of Effort" ($c_{\text{effort}}$) is the dominant inhibitor of compliance, accounting for roughly 83\% of output variance \citep{Yazawa2025}. Conversely, the "Attitude" parameter showed near-zero sensitivity, quantifying a distinct "Value-Action Gap" \citep{Zhao2022}. This proves that passive Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns (like flyers or radio announcements) will fail if the physical friction of segregation remains unaddressed \citep{Moeini2023}.

The LGU must transition its budget from awareness to physical convenience:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Subsidizing Receptacles in Low-Income Zones:} Interviews revealed that residents in areas like Liangan East find even basic sacks (\textit{sako}) too expensive to purchase for segregation. The LGU must allocate funds to distribute free, standardized bins to these vulnerable households.
    \item \textbf{Fixing Logistical Bottlenecks:} In rural and sprawling barangays, logistical failures force residents to rely on compost pits or illegal dumping. The LGU must prioritize hardware maintenance, such as repairing the damaged MultiCab in Barangay Liangan East, to ensure the physical act of waste disposal is as effortless as possible.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proof-of-Concept: Computational Prototyping for SWM Policy}
The "Bacolod Multi-View Simulation UI" developed in this study serves as a proof-of-concept for evidence-based governance. While the current iteration is a research prototype designed for computational experiments rather than deployable enterprise software, it demonstrates the profound utility of predictive modeling in public administration.

Traditionally, LGU appropriation ordinances are drafted based on static heuristics and intuition. This research proves the necessity of transitioning to a dynamic, "What-If" planning model. By commissioning or developing localized decision-support systems based on this DRL architecture, future administrators will be able to input demographic shifts, vehicle logistics, and budget constraints to simulate policy outcomes \textit{before} enacting municipal ordinances. This shifts the LGU from reactive enforcement to proactive, data-driven governance.

\section{Calibrated Enforcement and Political Capital Management}
The simulation unequivocally proved that a "Pure Enforcement" strategy is politically brittle, triggering a "Political Collapse" by Quarter 2 due to extreme public backlash. Heavy-handed penalization without adequate infrastructure traps the LGU in an unsustainable cycle of resentment. 

The proposed framework advises against uniform, draconian ticketing. Instead, MENRO should deploy citation tickets selectively during the initial "Saturation Phase" of a targeted barangay to break early resistance, and then immediately scale back enforcement once compliance rises, allowing Political Capital to recover to a stable 1.0000.

\section{Managing the 70\% Tipping Point and Habit Maintenance}
The HuDRL agent successfully discovered that behavior does not require perpetual, high-intensity funding once it becomes culturally normalized. The LGU must monitor barangays until they cross the "Social Tipping Point" of approximately 70\% compliance \citep{Centola2018}.

Once a barangay breaches this threshold, social norms ($\omega_{sn}$) naturally sustain the behavior \citep{Andre2021}. The policy recommendation here is the "Graduation Rule": upon reaching 70\% compliance, MENRO must drastically reduce that barangay's funding to a fractional "Maintenance Budget" tuned only to counter habit decay, freeing up massive capital to besiege the next resistant population center.

\section{Inter-Barangay Financial Restructuring}
Interview data revealed severe fiscal disparities, with some barangays operating on Solid Waste Management MOOE budgets as low as 15,000 PHP annually (e.g., Barangay Babalaya) while others exceed 200,000 PHP (e.g., Barangay Poblacion). The LGU must move away from static, predefined allocation ratios commonly found in annual appropriation ordinances \citep{Abdallah2021}. 

The smart governance framework proposes transitioning to a dynamic, performance-based funding system. The municipal government should pool resources to subsidize high-impact infrastructure (like localized Materials Recovery Facilities) in underfunded barangays based on the AI's quarterly triage recommendations, ensuring capital flows precisely where behavioral inertia is highest.
