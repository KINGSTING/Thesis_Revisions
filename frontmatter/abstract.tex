\begin{abstract}

The ongoing struggle with low household compliance regarding solid waste segregation, as mandated by the Philippine Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (R.A. 9003), significantly constrains the effectiveness of municipal solid waste management. While Local Government Units (LGUs) employ various incentives and penalties, these strategies often fail to account for the heterogeneous behaviors of households or the severe logistical and financial constraints of frontline barangay implementers. This study proposes a comprehensive Agent-Based Model (ABM) coupled with Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to optimize policy interventions within a decentralized, dual-budget governance architecture. Specifically, the simulation models the macro-level municipal LGU (acting as the Mayor Agent) strategically deploying a limited augmentation fund to rescue failing micro-level barangay baseline implementations in the Municipality of Bacolod, Lanao del Norte.

The ABM simulates household decision-making by integrating the Theory of Planned Behavior (Attitude, Subjective Norms, and Perceived Behavioral Control) with socio-demographic factors and spatial policy constraints. Simulation results revealed that the current "Status Quo" strategy of equitable resource distribution fails to overcome behavioral inertia. Global Sensitivity Analysis indicated that the physical "Cost of Effort" is the primary driver of non-compliance (accounting for 83\% of variance), while internal "Attitude" showed near-zero sensitivity, suggesting severe diminishing returns on pure Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns.

Crucially, the DRL agent autonomously discovered a novel "Sequential Saturation" strategy. The model demonstrated that sequentially concentrating municipal funds to trigger localized social norm "tipping points" (at approximately 70\% compliance) in specific barangays is vastly superior to a simultaneous, equal distribution across the municipality. The study concludes that optimal governance requires a dynamic, phased allocation of targeted enforcement and incentives to overcome logistical friction, providing LGUs with a robust, data-driven roadmap to effectively subsidize and enforce R.A. 9003.

\noindent \textbf{Keywords}---\textit{Household Waste Segregation, Agent-Based Modeling, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Theory of Planned Behavior, Dual-Budget Governance, Policy Optimization}

\end{abstract}
