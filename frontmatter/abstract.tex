\begin{abstract}

The persistent challenge of low household compliance with the Philippine Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (R.A. 9003) significantly hampers municipal solid waste operations. Existing Local Government Unit (LGU) strategies—which rely on static incentives and penalties—often fail to address heterogeneous household behaviors or the severe logistical and financial constraints faced by frontline barangay implementers. To address this gap, this study develops and computationally explores a framework coupling an Agent-Based Model (ABM) with Heuristic-Guided Deep Reinforcement Learning (HuDRL). The core objective is to evaluate the operational and optimization performance of this system in discovering the most efficient strategy for deploying a limited 1.5 million peso municipal augmentation budget within the Municipality of Bacolod, Lanao del Norte.

The ABM is explicitly designed as an abstract testbed for policy optimization, not a full ecological or psychological simulation. Household decision-making is modeled using a simplified utility function inspired by the Theory of Planned Behavior. Crucially, the psychometric parameters governing agent behavior were derived from structured Key Informant Interviews with barangay officials, rather than direct household psychometric surveys. Within this bounded environment, the HuDRL agent learns to allocate the fixed budget across enforcement, incentives, and information campaigns under a decentralized, dual-budget governance architecture.

Baseline simulations suggest that the prevailing "Status Quo" strategy of equitable resource distribution fails to overcome community-wide behavioral inertia. Global Sensitivity Analysis demonstrates within the model that the physical 'Cost of Effort' is the primary driver of non-compliance, accounting for approximately 79\% of the variance in simulated outcomes, while internal 'Attitude' exhibits near-zero direct sensitivity. Guided by safety-critical heuristics, the HuDRL agent successfully optimizes budget deployment by adopting a "Sequential Saturation" strategy. The simulation suggests that concentrating municipal funds to trigger localized social norm "tipping points" (at approximately 70\% compliance) in one barangay before rotating to the next is mathematically optimal. Within these constraints, the HuDRL agent significantly outperforms static heuristics, achieving a terminal compliance of 90.45\% in simulation.

It must be emphasized that these findings are conditional on the synthesized behavioral parameter ranges. What this model demonstrates is the operational efficacy of the HuDRL system in resource-constrained environments; it does not predict real-world psychological change or long-term ecological outcomes outside its parameter bounds. Ultimately, this study provides LGUs with a scientifically grounded, data-driven roadmap, suggesting that optimal governance necessitates dynamic, phased allocations of targeted subsidies.

\noindent \textbf{Keywords}---\textit{Household Waste Segregation, Agent-Based Modeling, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Theory of Planned Behavior, Dual-Budget Governance, Policy Optimization}

\end{abstract}
