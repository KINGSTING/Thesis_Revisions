\begin{abstract}

The persistent challenge of low household compliance with the Philippine Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (R.A. 9003) significantly hampers municipal solid waste operations. Existing Local Government Unit (LGU) strategies—which rely on static incentives and penalties—often fail to address heterogeneous household behaviors or the severe logistical and financial constraints faced by frontline barangay implementers. To address this gap, this study proposes a novel framework coupling an Agent-Based Model (ABM) with Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to optimize policy interventions within a decentralized, dual-budget governance architecture. Specifically, the simulation models a macro-level municipal LGU (acting as the Mayor Agent) strategically deploying a limited augmentation fund to support struggling micro-level barangay programs in the Municipality of Bacolod, Lanao del Norte.

The ABM captures household decision-making by integrating the Theory of Planned Behavior (Attitude, Subjective Norms, and Perceived Behavioral Control) with socio-demographic factors and spatial constraints. Baseline simulations reveal that the prevailing ``Status Quo'' strategy of equitable resource distribution fails to overcome community-wide behavioral inertia. Furthermore, Global Sensitivity Analysis demonstrates that the physical ``Cost of Effort'' is the primary driver of non-compliance, accounting for 83\% of the variance. Conversely, internal ``Attitude'' exhibited near-zero sensitivity, indicating severe diminishing returns on traditional Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns.

Crucially, guided by safety-critical heuristics, the DRL agent successfully optimized the precise temporal execution of a "Sequential Saturation" strategy. The model proves that sequentially concentrating municipal funds to trigger localized social norm "tipping points" (at approximately 70\% compliance) in specific barangays is vastly superior to a simultaneous, equal distribution across the municipality. The study concludes that optimal governance necessitates a dynamic, phased allocation of targeted enforcement and logistical subsidies to overcome friction, providing LGUs with a robust, data-driven roadmap for effective R.A. 9003 implementation.

\noindent \textbf{Keywords}---\textit{Household Waste Segregation, Agent-Based Modeling, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Theory of Planned Behavior, Dual-Budget Governance, Policy Optimization}

\end{abstract}
