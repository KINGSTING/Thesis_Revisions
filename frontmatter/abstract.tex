\begin{abstract}

The ongoing struggle with low household compliance regarding solid waste segregation rules, as mandated by the Philippine Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (RA 9003), significantly constrains the effectiveness of municipal solid waste management. While Local Government Units (LGUs) employ various incentives and penalties, these strategies often fail to account for the heterogeneous behaviors of households or their long-term economic impact. This study proposes a comprehensive Agent-Based Model (ABM) and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework to identify the optimal policy mix for maximizing segregation compliance in the Municipality of Bacolod, Lanao del Norte. The ABM simulates household decision-making by integrating the Theory of Planned Behavior (Attitude, Subjective Norms, and Perceived Behavioral Control) with socio-demographic factors and policy constraints. Simulation results revealed that the current "Status Quo" strategy of equitable resource distribution fails to overcome behavioral inertia in high-density urban areas. Global Sensitivity Analysis indicated that the "Cost of Effort" is the primary driver of non-compliance (83\% variance), while "Attitude" showed near-zero sensitivity, suggesting diminishing returns on Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns.

The DRL agent autonomously discovered a novel "Sequential Saturation" strategy, demonstrating that sequentially concentrating resources to trigger social norm "tipping points" (at approx. 70\% compliance) is superior to simultaneous equal distribution. The study concludes that optimal governance requires a dynamic, phased allocation of enforcement and incentives to overcome logistical friction, providing LGUs with a robust, data-driven roadmap for policy optimization.

\noindent \textbf{Keywords}---\textit{Household Waste Segregation, Agent-Based Modeling, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Theory of Planned Behavior, Policy Optimization, Local Governance}

\end{abstract}
