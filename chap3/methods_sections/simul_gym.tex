This section details the experimental design, outlining the operationalization of the coupled ABM-RL model to address the core research questions. This framework defines how the simulation was executed and measured across four key stages: (1) model initialization, calibration, and training; (2) financial constraints and cost estimations; (3) the design of tested policy scenarios; and (4) performance metrics used to evaluate the outcomes.

This study is models a Direct Intervention \& Augmentation, where the Local Government Unit (LGU) intervene and augments the failing base implementation of the Barangay Local Government Unit (BLGU) as mandated in Section 17 of the Local Goverment Code of 1991 (R.A. 7160). Both municipal and barangay funds programmed and appropriated for Solid Waste Management to be used in the 3 policy levers (i.e. Information \& Education Campaigns, Enforcement, and Incentives).

\subsection{Policy Scenarios and Associated Resource Constraints}

The approach of testing distinct, constrained policy scenarios is a standard and necessary step in Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) studies embedded within Agent-Based Models, allowing researchers to isolate the causal impact of different instruments on system outcomes \citep{Jimenez2025, TianReview2024}. To systematically evaluate policy efficacy and address the chronic lack of funding often cited as a root cause of institutional failure in waste management \citep{Ibanez2022, Santos2025}, four core scenarios were simulated. These scenarios manipulate the available action space across three municipal levers—Information/Education, Enforcement, and Incentives—while tracking the depletion of a \textpeso 1,500,000 annual municipal fund.

The first scenario is the \textbf{Baseline - Status Quo (PURE IEC)}, which restricts the action space so that resources are entirely dedicated to Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns. Relying purely on public awareness and intrinsic motivation, it serves as a foundational benchmark \citep{Collado2024}. To accurately reflect the operational realities gathered from key informant interviews, the cost and intensity of IEC are bifurcated into two distinct administrative levels: the Barangay level (Micro-IEC) and the Municipal level (Macro-IEC).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Barangay Level: Micro-IEC (Grassroots Campaigns):} 
    At the barangay level, limited solid waste management budgets necessitate grassroots, highly localized communication strategies. Based on field interviews, barangays primarily utilize \textit{Recoridas} (roving vehicle announcements) and \textit{Purok} (neighborhood) meetings to cascade information \citep{appendix:ezperanza_interview, appendix:liangan_interview}. The Micro-IEC intensity ($I_{micro}$) is modeled by prioritizing the most cost-effective, wide-reaching methods first. Let $B_{iec}$ be the barangay's IEC budget constraint, with $C_{rec}$ and $C_{pur}$ representing the unit costs of a Recorida and a Purok meeting, respectively:

\begin{align}
    n_{rec} &= \min \left( T_{rec}, \left\lfloor \frac{B_{iec}}{C_{rec}} \right\rfloor \right) \label{eq:micro_recorida} \\
    rem_{iec} &= \max \left( 0, B_{iec} - (n_{rec} \times C_{rec}) \right) \label{eq:micro_rem} \\
    n_{pur} &= \min \left( T_{pur}, \left\lfloor \frac{rem_{iec}}{C_{pur}} \right\rfloor \right) \label{eq:micro_purok} \\
    I_{micro} &= \min \left( 1.0, \omega_{rec} \left( \frac{n_{rec}}{T_{rec}} \right) + \omega_{pur} \left( \frac{n_{pur}}{T_{pur}} \right) \right) \label{eq:micro_intensity}
\end{align}
\addequation{Cost and Intensity of Barangay-Level Micro-IEC}

    Where $T_{rec}$ and $T_{pur}$ are the target frequencies per quarter, $rem_{iec}$ is the residual budget, and $\omega_{rec}, \omega_{pur}$ represent the proportional weight of each activity on behavioral impact. The use of the floor function ($\lfloor x \rfloor$) ensures discrete procurement of services, while the minimum function bounds the intensity, reflecting diminishing returns once target saturation is achieved.

    \item \textbf{Municipal Level: Macro-IEC (Mass Media Campaigns):} 
    Conversely, the Local Government Unit (LGU) possesses the financial capacity to execute macro-level mass media campaigns. According to the Municipal Environment and Natural Resources Office (MENRO), the LGU's most effective tools are local radio broadcasts (e.g., 101.3 Grace Covenant FM) and large-scale mobilization events \citep{appendix:menro_interview}. The Macro-IEC intensity ($I_{macro}$) models this tiered fixed-cost procurement:

\begin{align}
    n_{rad} &= \min \left( T_{rad}, \left\lfloor \frac{B_{lgu\_iec}}{C_{rad}} \right\rfloor \right) \label{eq:macro_radio} \\
    rem_{lgu} &= \max \left( 0, B_{lgu\_iec} - (n_{rad} \times C_{rad}) \right) \label{eq:macro_rem} \\
    n_{evt} &= \min \left( T_{evt}, \left\lfloor \frac{rem_{lgu}}{C_{evt}} \right\rfloor \right) \label{eq:macro_event} \\
    I_{macro} &= \min \left( 1.0, \omega_{rad} \left( \frac{n_{rad}}{T_{rad}} \right) + \omega_{evt} \left( \frac{n_{evt}}{T_{evt}} \right) \right) \label{eq:macro_intensity}
\end{align}
\addequation{Cost and Intensity of Municipal-Level Macro-IEC}

    In these equations, $B_{lgu\_iec}$ represents the allocated municipal intervention fund, and $C_{rad}$ and $C_{evt}$ are the respective activity costs. By structuring the equations in this tiered format, the model enforces realistic budget constraints on the reinforcement learning agent, preventing infinite scaling of public attitude through unrealistic over-spending \citep{TianReview2024}.
\end{itemize}

The second scenario, \textbf{Pure Enforcement}, prioritizes monitoring, fines, and institutional coercion to simulate a strict ``No Segregation, No Collection'' mandate \citep{Wang2023, Dalugdog2021}. Enforcement expenditure ($C_{\mathrm{Enf}}$) is calculated as a function of the Coverage Ratio. Assuming a single officer monitors a maximum of 30 households daily to account for travel and administrative burdens \citep{Nishimura2022}, the cost is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    C_{\mathrm{Enf}} = (N_{\mathrm{Enforcers}} \times W_{\mathrm{Daily}} \times 66)
    \label{eq:cost_enforcement}
\end{equation}
\addequation{Cost of Enforcement Function}
\noindent Where $N_{\mathrm{Enforcers}}$ is the required personnel (determining the Probability of Detection), $W_{\mathrm{Daily}}$ is the regional minimum daily wage for Region X, and 66 represents the quarterly working days.

The third scenario, \textbf{Pure Incentives}, allocates funds exclusively toward positive reinforcement, rewards, and behavioral nudges \citep{LoanBalanay2023, Chen2023, MuZhang2021, Vorobeva2022}. The cost ($C_{\mathrm{Inc}}$) acts as a Variable Success Function, introducing a critical "Victim of Success" risk where high behavioral compliance increases the municipality's financial liability \citep{WorldBank2022}:
\begin{equation}
    C_{\mathrm{Inc}} = (V_{\mathrm{Reward}} \times N_{\mathrm{Compliant}})
    \label{eq:cost_incentives}
\end{equation}
\addequation{Cost of Incentives Function}
\noindent Where $V_{\mathrm{Reward}}$ is the monetary value of the incentive and $N_{\mathrm{Compliant}}$ is the count of compliant households.

Finally, the \textbf{Heuristic-Guided Deep Reinforcement Learning} scenario represents an optimized, adaptive framework where the AI agent accesses its full action space. It dynamically allocates funds across IEC, Enforcement, and Incentives simultaneously in response to shifting behaviors. Stabilized by expert heuristics to prevent physically impossible resource allocations, this approach seeks an optimal, long-term budget strategy that balances maximum compliance with fiscal sustainability \citep{MuZhang2021, Zhao2022, TianReview2024}.

\subsection{The Simulation Main Loop Algorithm}
The simulation operates on a highly structured dual timescale designed to capture both micro-level human behavior and macro-level administrative governance \citep{Brugiere2022}. At the micro-level, the environment processes daily ticks where individual agents---such as households and enforcement personnel---execute their routine interactions, evaluate their behavioral friction, and make daily segregation decisions \citep{Meng2018}.  Concurrently, the macro-level operates on a 90-day (quarterly) epoch, which serves as the fundamental unit of analysis and adaptation for the governance agents \citep{Akbarpour2021}. This quarterly interval triggers the central policy evaluation, allowing the Local Government Unit (LGU) to observe the accumulated socio-behavioral shifts from the daily ticks and adapt its resource allocation strategy for the subsequent cycle to maximize long-term compliance \citep{Jimenez2025}.

The main simulation loop is driven by two primary inputs: the \textit{Annual Budget} and \textit{Total Days}. The \textit{Annual Budget} establishes the strict fiscal constraints of the municipality, forcing the policy engine to solve the multi-objective optimization problem without violating hard financial limits \citep{Abdallah2021}. The \textit{Total Days} parameter defines the finite temporal horizon of the simulation (e.g., 1080 ticks representing a 3-year period), aligning the Markov Decision Process (MDP) with the standard political term of local government officials \citep{Akbarpour2021}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Main Simulation Loop}
\label{alg:main_loop_english}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Starting Data Needed:} Annual Budget, Total Number of Days (e.g., 1080 days for 3 years)
\State \textbf{Initial Setup:} Set up the starting environment and bring all participants into the simulation (Households, Enforcers, Barangays, and Mayor Agent)
\Statex
\State \textbf{The Daily Routine:}
\For{every single day from day 1 to the end of the simulation}
    \Statex
    \State \textbf{1. Check for the End of the Quarter}
    \If{today marks the end of a quarter (every 90 days)}
        \State Add +1 to the Quarter counter
        \Statex
        \State \textit{The Mayor Agent: Make Budget Decisions}
        \State Look at the current overall situation in the simulation
        \State Calculate how the budget should be divided based on what is happening
        \State Put those budget decisions into action
        \Statex
        \State \textit{The Household Agents: Redemption Status Resets}
        \For{every single household}
            \State Reset their redemption status back to False
        \EndFor
    \EndIf
    \Statex
    \State \textbf{2. Perform Daily Activities}
    \For{each Barangay}
        \State Perform its standard daily routine
    \EndFor
    \For{each individual participant (Households and Enforcers)}
        \State Perform their daily actions and make their daily choices
    \EndFor
    \Statex
    \State \textbf{3. End-of-Day System Maintenance}
    \State Update the overall Political Capital based on what happened today
    \State Calculate the daily operating costs and deduct them
    \State Collect and save the day's data and performance metrics
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

A critical mechanism embedded within this loop is the quarterly \textit{Redemption Status Reset}. By reverting every household's redemption status to False at the start of a new 90-day epoch, the simulation prevents the indefinite accumulation of rewards based on historical actions. This mechanism mirrors real-world administrative cycles, forcing households to actively renew their compliance behaviors (such as consistent waste segregation) in the current quarter to qualify for municipal incentives. Consequently, this ensures that the Mayor Agent's newly allocated incentive budget strictly targets and measures contemporaneous compliance rather than subsidizing past behavior.

Crucially, this loop serves as the structural bridge connecting the descriptive Agent-Based Model (ABM) environment to the prescriptive Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policy engine \citep{Kompella2020}. As the loop executes, the ABM acts as a high-fidelity data generator, accumulating the complex, non-linear compliance behaviors of heterogeneous household agents \citep{TianReview2024}. At each 90-day epoch, these behavioral observations are aggregated into a multi-dimensional state vector and transmitted to the RL agent, which then processes this environmental feedback to calculate a newly optimized policy action—specifically, the dynamic reallocation of funds across incentives, enforcement, and educational campaigns \citep{HaMinh2025}.
