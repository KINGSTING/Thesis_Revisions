To address the high-dimensional resource allocation problem inherent in municipal budgeting, this study develops a \textbf{Heuristic-Guided Deep Reinforcement Learning (HuDRL)} framework using the PyTorch library Stable Baselines3 \citep{Raffin2021}. This architecture utilizes Deep Proximal Policy Optimization (Deep PPO) to approximate an optimal governance policy $\pi_\theta(a|s)$ within a continuous action space.

\subsection{Addressing Reward Sparsity and HuDRL Architecture}

A fundamental challenge in applying Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to complex governance systems is the \textit{Sparse Reward Problem}. The resource allocation task involves a high-dimensional continuous action space ($d=21$), representing the simultaneous adjustment of three policy levers (IEC, Enforcement, Incentives) across seven distinct barangays. 

In a standard, unguided PPO environment, an agent effectively wanders randomly, rarely discovering that concentrating massive resources on a single barangay—the ``Sequential Saturation'' strategy—is the key to breaking behavioral inertia. Instead, agents frequently converge to a ``Status Quo'' local optimum characterized by thin, equitable resource distribution that fails to trigger behavioral tipping points. To overcome this, the HuDRL framework integrates domain-specific heuristics to guide exploration toward economically and physically viable strategies \citep{ZhengAI2022}.


To operationalize this, a custom Actor-Critic network was developed to process the multi-modal state vector of the municipality. Both the Actor (policy) and the Critic (value) networks utilize two fully connected hidden layers with 64 neurons each, employing Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation functions to capture the non-linear relationships between enforcement intensity and household behavioral responses \citep{Dey2025}. The Actor network culminates in a Softmax activation function, which transforms raw neural logits into a normalized probability distribution, mathematically guaranteeing that the sum of all budget allocations strictly adheres to the municipality's fiscal limits \citep{Jimenez2025}.

\subsection{RL Formulation: State, Action, and Reward Spaces}

\subsubsection{State Representation \texorpdfstring{($S_t$)}{(St)}}
The state space $S_t$ constitutes the comprehensive observation vector accessible to the LGU agent at the onset of each decision epoch $t$, formalized as:
\begin{equation}
    S_t = [\mathbf{CB}_{1...7}, B_{\mathrm{Rem}}, M_{\mathrm{Index}}, P_{\mathrm{Cap}}]
    \label{eq:state_vector}
\end{equation}
\addequation{State Vector Representation}

\noindent This includes the real-time compliance rate for each constituent barangay ($\mathbf{CB}_{1...7}$), the remaining balance of the annual fund ($B_{\mathrm{Rem}}$), the current fiscal quarter representing the MDP's temporal horizon ($M_{\mathrm{Index}}$), and Political Capital ($P_{\mathrm{Cap}}$). Political Capital acts as a dynamic constraint on the admissibility of punitive measures, evolving based on enforcement intensity ($E_{\mathrm{Intensity}}$) and natural recovery over time ($T_{\mathrm{Decay}}$) \citep{Nishimura2022}:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{Cap}(t+1)} = P_{\mathrm{Cap}(t)} - (\alpha \cdot E_{\mathrm{Intensity}}) + (\beta \cdot T_{\mathrm{Decay}})
    \label{eq:political_capital}
\end{equation}
\addequation{Political Capital Transition Function}

\subsubsection{Action Space \texorpdfstring{($A_t$)}{(At)} and Budget Constraint}
The continuous action space ($A_t \in \mathbb{R}^{21}$) represents the simultaneous allocation of resources across IEC, Enforcement, and Incentives for all seven barangays. To enforce fiscal viability, the raw output ($A_{\mathrm{raw}}$) is processed through a Softmax normalization layer and scaled by the Quarterly Budget Cap ($B_{\mathrm{Quarterly}}$):
\begin{equation}
   A_{\mathrm{final}} = \mathrm{Softmax}(A_{\mathrm{raw}}) \cdot B_{\mathrm{Quarterly}}
   \label{eq:softmax_constraint}
\end{equation}
\addequation{Action Space Softmax Normalization Constraint}

This mathematically guarantees that the summation of all allocated funds adheres strictly to the hard budgetary constraint ($\sum_{i=1}^{21} A_{t,i} \le B_{\mathrm{Quarterly}}$), compelling the agent to optimize for cost-effectiveness within a recurring fiscal framework \citep{Abdallah2021}.

\subsubsection{Multi-Objective Reward Function \texorpdfstring{($R_{\mathrm{total}}$)}{(Rtotal)}}
The agent is guided by a Composite Reward Function balancing environmental compliance, fiscal constraints, and social acceptability:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{total}} = w_1 R_{\mathrm{Comp}} + w_2 R_{\mathrm{Sustain}} - w_3 P_{\mathrm{Backlash}} + R_{\mathrm{Shaping}}
    \label{eq:total_reward}
\end{equation}
\addequation{Composite Multi-Objective Reward Function}

This balances the population-weighted waste segregation rate ($R_{\mathrm{Comp}}$), penalizes premature budget exhaustion ($R_{\mathrm{Sustain}}$), and applies a non-linear penalty ($P_{\mathrm{Backlash}}$) for draconian policies that trigger social resistance. Crucially, it includes shaping rewards ($R_{\mathrm{Shaping}}$) to accelerate learning, which is tightly integrated with the system's heuristic logic.

\subsection{The HuDRL Algorithm: Triage Logic and Targeted Amplification}

To accelerate convergence and ground the AI in realistic administrative practices, the agent's raw continuous output is modulated by a heuristic Triage Logic \citep{Alphonso2024} before normalization. This functions as a saliency filter, evaluating the compliance gap of each geographic unit and forcefully categorizing barangays into three strategic states: Attack, Sustain (Solved), and Queue. 

A critical feature of this triage logic is the ``Too Big To Fail'' safety net applied to major population centers like Barangay Poblacion. The algorithm grants Poblacion significantly higher baseline multipliers to ensure a steady stream of maintenance funding, reflecting the logistical reality that central urban hubs cannot be ignored without mathematically devastating the global compliance score \citep{Abordo2025}.

This algorithm forces the agent to explore the "saturation" strategy of overwhelming a single area with resources. The learning process is reinforced through a discrete ``Jackpot Bonus'' ($+20,000$) if the agent successfully allocates more than 40\% of the quarterly budget to the critical barangay. This artificial feedback signal effectively teaches the agent that focused intervention yields higher long-term returns than equitable distribution.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{AI Decision Process: Prioritizing Barangays and Shaping Rewards}
\label{alg:hudrl_triage_english}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Starting Information Needed:} The AI's initial budget guess, the list of all Barangays, and the available Quarterly Budget
\State \textbf{End Goal:} A finalized, highly optimized budget distribution plan
\Statex
\State \textbf{Step 1: Evaluate the Current Situation}
\State Set the target "Passing Grade" to 70\% compliance
\State Find the ``Weakest Link'' (the single Barangay with the lowest compliance rate right now)
\State Make a list of ``Solved'' Barangays (those that have already passed the 70\% target)
\Statex
\State \textbf{Step 2: Apply the Triage Strategy (Prioritizing Funds)}
\For{every single Barangay on the list}
    \Statex
    \State \textit{ATTACK MODE: Targeted Amplification}
    \If{this Barangay is the ``Weakest Link''}
        \State Massively multiply its proposed share of the budget (focus resources here)
    \Statex
    \State \textit{SUSTAIN MODE: Maintenance Funding}
    \ElsIf{this Barangay is already on the ``Solved'' list}
        \State Give it a moderate maintenance budget (with a slight bonus if it is the central ``Poblacion'' district)
    \Statex
    \State \textit{QUEUE MODE: Waiting Their Turn}
    \Else
        \State Give it almost no budget for now so funds aren't spread too thin (unless it is ``Poblacion'')
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\Statex
\State \textbf{Step 3: Finalize the Budget}
\State Adjust all these multiplied shares mathematically so they perfectly equal the actual Quarterly Budget limit
\State Apply this final budget to the simulation (funding Information Campaigns, Enforcers, and Incentives)
\Statex
\State \textbf{Step 4: Grade the AI's Performance (Reward Shaping)}
\State Start with zero bonus points for the AI
\State \textit{Did the AI learn the triage strategy?}
\If{the AI successfully assigned more than 40\% of the entire budget to the ``Weakest Link''}
    \State Give the AI a massive ``Jackpot'' bonus of +20,000 points
    \State \textit{This teaches the AI the value of solving one major problem at a time (Sequential Saturation)}
\EndIf
\State Add this bonus to the AI's standard score for the quarter
\Statex
\State \textbf{Step 5: AI Learning Update}
\State Update the AI's programming based on the situation it saw, the budget it created, and the total score it earned
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
