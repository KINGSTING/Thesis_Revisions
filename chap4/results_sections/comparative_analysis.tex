The results from these simulations unequivocally demonstrated that without the ``Sequential Saturation'' logic discovered by the artificial intelligence, no amount of static rebalancing between Education, Enforcement, or Incentives was sufficient to resolve the municipality-wide non-compliance problem \citep{Jimenez2025, ZhengAI2022}.

\subsection{Baseline: The Status Quo}
The baseline scenario replicated the current administrative strategy employed by the Local Government Unit (LGU), wherein the limited municipal budget was distributed equally among all seven barangays. This resulted in a fixed allocation of exactly \textpeso 53,571.43 per quarter for each unit. As evidenced by the initial data (Quarter 1), this strategy heavily prioritized Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials---absorbing between 76.3\% and 88.9\% of the local allocations---under the assumption that passive awareness campaigns would naturally drive and sustain behavioral change regarding solid waste management \citep{Abordo2025}. The detailed financial and compliance data tracking this scenario over the 3-year term for each barangay can be found in Appendix \ref{ch:appendix_c}.

The simulation results by Quarter 12 (Tick 990) revealed that this equal-distribution strategy is highly ineffective at maintaining long-term participation. Rather than stabilizing, the compliance rates decayed significantly over the simulation period, as illustrated by the stark drop-offs in the community averages.

The localized data completely dismantled the assumption that passive education is sufficient for habit formation \citep{Abdallah2021}. By the end of the simulation, the vast majority of the municipality exhibited severe behavioral relapse. Only a few areas managed to retain partial participation---most notably Barangay Babalaya, which maintained a 49.71\% compliance rate by the final quarters---relying on inherent social momentum within its population rather than LGU enforcement. Meanwhile, previously active communities flatlined; for instance, Barangay Liangan East dropped from an initial 14.47\% compliance in Quarter 1 down to negligible levels, and Barangay Mati zeroed out entirely to 0.00\% compliance by the end of the term.

Most critically, this strategy triggered a catastrophic failure in the municipality's core zones. The failure of the ``Status Quo'' demonstrated that an equal distribution strategy acts as a severe dilution mechanism. By spreading resources evenly across all barangays regardless of population density or waste generation rates, the LGU failed to generate the threshold intensity required to break behavioral resistance. This outcome strongly validates the equity-efficiency tradeoff observed in algorithmic resource allocation \citep{Alphonso2024}, proving that without concentrated, localized interventions (such as targeted enforcement or dynamic incentives), high-density areas default to non-compliance despite the steady funding of basic educational campaigns.

\subsection{Pure Enforcement Strategy}

In the second scenario, the entire LGU allocation was shifted toward hiring Enforcement Agents (Tanods) while maintaining the principle of equal distribution. This strategy tested the Deterrence Hypothesis, which posits that increasing the probability of detection through stricter policing is the most effective driver of compliance \citep{Chen2023}. The comprehensive quarterly results for this pure enforcement strategy across all seven barangays are tabulated in Appendix \ref{sec:pure_enforcement}.

Contrary to the hypothesis that strict policing universally drives compliance, the simulation revealed that the Pure Enforcement strategy yielded highly polarized results across the municipality. While the true population-weighted global compliance stabilized at approximately 29.60\% by Quarter 12, this average masked extreme local variations. Smaller or more manageable areas such as Barangay Binuni (98.82\%) and Barangay Ezperanza (97.04\%) rapidly achieved and sustained near-total compliance, while Barangay Babalaya stabilized at a moderate 52.05\%.

However, the strategy failed critically in other zones. The municipality's core and high-density areas, including Barangay Poblacion (0.13\%), Barangay Demologan (1.51\%), and Barangay Mati (0.00\%), remained completely stagnant near zero. Notably, Barangay Liangan East experienced early behavioral shifts but eventually decayed, settling at a negligible 5.76\% compliance rate by the end of the simulation.

This severe disparity reveals a critical insight regarding the economics of coercion within an equal distribution model: enforcement efficacy is highly sensitive to the specific demographic parameters of each locale. In areas like Binuni, the allocated agents likely exceeded the critical density threshold required to make non-compliance perceived as highly risky \citep{Hertweck2023}. Conversely, in highly populated or critical areas like Poblacion, or areas prone to behavior decay like Liangan East, the equal distribution of resources resulted in severely diluted enforcement. The policing density never reached the necessary threshold to deter non-compliance effectively.

Furthermore, the total absence of Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns and monetary incentives eliminated any mechanism to improve the citizens' intrinsic Attitude or Perceived Behavioral Control. This created a purely punitive system that failed to initiate or sustain positive behavior in the majority of the municipality, aligning with the findings of \citet{Badua2022}, who highlighted that strictly punitive measures are often ineffective and prone to rapid behavioral relapse if not supported by broader psychological and infrastructure implementation strategies.

\subsection{Pure Incentives Strategy}

The third scenario allocated the majority of municipal funds toward providing monetary rewards to households, testing the efficacy of a purely positive reinforcement model. When looking at the simulation's visual output, there appears to be a disconnect between the towering success of individual localized areas and the rather subdued trajectory shown on the municipality-wide graph. As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:pure_incentives_graph}, the true, population-weighted global compliance rate only reached a modest 32.20\% by Quarter 12. This graph perfectly captures the mathematical reality of "budget dilution" across a municipality with uneven population distributions.

This outcome validated the finding by \citet{Camarillo2021} that economic incentives require a strict \textit{saturation point} to be effective; if the monetary reward does not cross a minimum threshold of perceived value, it completely fails to trigger the desired behavioral shift.

\subsection{Heuristic-Guided Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach}

The Deep Reinforcement Learning (PPO) agent, operating under the "Heuristic-Guided" framework, completely outperformed all manual baseline strategies by discovering a highly non-linear resource allocation logic termed \textit{Sequential Saturation}. The complete quarterly financial and compliance data demonstrating the success of this dynamic approach for each barangay are tabulated in Appendix \ref{sec:hudrl_results}.

Unlike the manual strategies that plateaued or failed catastrophically in high-density areas, the AI-driven strategy achieved near-universal participation by Quarter 12. As established in the simulation outputs, the true, population-weighted global compliance rate surged to 98.23\% by the end of the simulation. Most notably, the strategy successfully cracked the behavioral resistance of Barangay Poblacion—the municipality's largest and most difficult demographic—driving it from a baseline of 0.00\% to an exceptional 98.76\% compliance rate.

The superior performance of this algorithm is attributed directly to its complete abandonment of the "Equal Distribution" heuristic. The simulation logs revealed that the AI did not attempt to fight the behavioral battle on all fronts simultaneously. Instead, it concentrated the vast majority of the total municipal budget into targeted, asymmetric bursts. The raw data shows the agent dynamically shifting up to nearly 99.94\% of the LGU's resources onto a single barangay for a dedicated period, fully saturating the psychological thresholds of the residents before rotating its focus to the next target area.

By strategically ignoring already compliant or highly resistant zones during certain quarters to focus overwhelming capital on a single tipping point, the PPO agent optimized the municipal budget far beyond human administrative baselines.

This "King of the Hill" logic is starkly evident in the sequential budget spikes recorded in the financial logs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quarter 1 (The Poblacion Strike):} The agent immediately dumped a massive \textbf{99.94\%} of the LGU's total quarterly budget directly into Barangay Poblacion, initiating the behavioral shift in the most critical zone right out of the gate.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 2 (The Demologan Siege):} The agent abruptly pivoted, allocating \textbf{68.92\%} of the budget to Barangay Demologan to raise its compliance to functional stability.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 3 (The Liangan Pivot):} Once Demologan was secured, the agent shifted \textbf{64.52\%} of funds to Barangay Liangan East.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 4 (The Mati Surge):} The focus rotated to Barangay Mati, receiving \textbf{60.64\%} of the quarterly allocation.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 5 (The Ezperanza Shift):} Funds were redirected to Barangay Ezperanza, capturing \textbf{57.20\%} of the budget.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 6 (The Babalaya Operation):} Barangay Babalaya received \textbf{54.08\%} of the allocation.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 7 (The Binuni Targeted):} The agent allocated \textbf{51.15\%} to Barangay Binuni.
    \item \textbf{Quarters 8--12 (The Poblacion Endgame):} Finally, identifying Barangay Poblacion as requiring sustained pressure to fully overcome the Urban Resource Trap, the agent committed a steady \textbf{42.85\%} of the budget for five consecutive quarters to break its inertia and achieve the final 98.76\% compliance mark.
\end{itemize}

This strategy mirrors the \textit{AI Economist} approach of discovering non-intuitive governance policies that human administrators typically avoid due to perceived unfairness \citep{ZhengAI2022}. The AI learned that by saturating one community until it reached a self-sustaining level of compliance (driven by localized social norms), it could safely withdraw funds and move the "siege" to the next non-compliant barangay without causing a collapse in previous gains \citep{Constantino2022}. This finding forcefully validates the hypothesis of a \textit{Social Tipping Point} \citep{Centola2018, Nyborg2024}, suggesting that true effectiveness in resource-constrained local governments requires the political will to prioritize sequential efficacy over simultaneous but ineffectual fairness.
