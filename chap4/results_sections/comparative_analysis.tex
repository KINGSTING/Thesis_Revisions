The results from these simulations unequivocally demonstrated that without the ``Sequential Saturation'' logic discovered by the artificial intelligence, no amount of static rebalancing between Education, Enforcement, or Incentives was sufficient to resolve the municipality-wide non-compliance problem \citep{Jimenez2025, ZhengAI2022}.

\subsection{Baseline: The Status Quo}

The baseline scenario replicated the current administrative strategy employed by the Local Government Unit (LGU), wherein the limited municipal budget was distributed equally among all seven barangays, resulting in an allocation of approximately \textpeso 53,571 per quarter for each unit. This strategy heavily prioritized IEC materials under the assumption that increasing awareness would drive behavioral change \citep{Paigalan2025}. 

The simulation results by Quarter 12 revealed a highly stratified outcome strictly determined by the initial demographic profile. Small, socially cohesive areas such as Barangay Mati and Barangay Babalaya achieved 100\% compliance, driven not by the LGU's funding but by the inherent social momentum within their tight-knit populations \citep{Yazawa2025}.

However, this apparent success in smaller communities masked a catastrophic failure in the municipality's critical zones. Barangay Poblacion, the commercial center and largest generator of waste, stagnated at a negligible 0.33\% compliance rate. This bifurcation confirmed the \textit{Urban Resource Trap} hypothesis, where high population density and anonymity dissolved the social pressure that facilitated compliance in rural areas \citep{Nishimura2022}. The failure of the ``Status Quo'' demonstrated that an equal distribution strategy acted as a \textit{dilution mechanism}; by spreading resources thinly across all areas, the LGU failed to generate the threshold intensity required to break behavioral resistance. This validated the assessment by \citet{Villanueva2021} that without strict, concentrated monitoring, high-density areas defaulted to non-compliance despite the presence of legal frameworks.

\subsection{Pure Enforcement Strategy}

In the second scenario, the entire LGU allocation was shifted toward hiring Enforcement Agents (Tanods) while maintaining the principle of equal distribution. This strategy tested the \textit{Deterrence Hypothesis}, which posited that increasing the probability of detection through stricter policing was the most effective driver of compliance \citep{Chen2023}.

Contrary to this hypothesis, the Pure Enforcement strategy performed the worst overall. While it had a marginal impact, critical areas saw a collapse in participation: Barangay Poblacion remained stagnant at 0.00\%. The data revealed a critical insight regarding the economics of coercion: \textit{diluted enforcement was effectively useless}. Even with 100\% of the funds allocated to police, the equal distribution model meant that Poblacion's enforcement intensity never reached the critical threshold required to make non-compliance perceived as risky \citep{Hertweck2023}. 

Furthermore, the total absence of IEC and incentives eliminated any mechanism to improve the agents' Attitude or Perceived Behavioral Control, creating a purely punitive system that failed to sustain positive behavior. This limitation aligned with the findings of \citet{Badua2022}, who highlighted that strictly punitive measures were often ineffective and unsustainable if not supported by broader implementation strategies.

\subsection{Pure Incentives Strategy}

The third scenario allocated all municipal funds to providing monetary rewards, testing the efficacy of a purely positive reinforcement model. The results demonstrated moderate success in smaller demographics (e.g., Barangay Babalaya reached 88.89\%) but ultimately failed in larger populations due to the mechanics of ``budget dilution.''

Rational agents simply ignored the microscopic reward as it did not offset the effort required to segregate waste \citep{Zhao2022}. This outcome validated the finding by \citet{Camarillo2021} that economic incentives required a \textit{saturation point} to be effective; if the reward did not meet a minimum threshold of value, it failed to trigger the desired behavioral shift.

\subsection{Heuristic-Guided Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach}

The Deep Reinforcement Learning (PPO) agent, operating under the ``Heuristic-Guided'' framework, completely outperformed all manual strategies by discovering a non-linear resource allocation logic termed \textbf{Sequential Saturation}. Unlike the manual strategies that plateaued or failed in high-density areas, the AI-driven strategy achieved near-universal compliance by Quarter 12, with even the difficult Barangay Poblacion reaching \textbf{98.76\%} compliance, while global averages exceeded 90\%.

The superior performance of this algorithm was attributed to its abandonment of the ``Equal Distribution'' heuristic. The simulation logs revealed that the AI did not fight on all fronts simultaneously; instead, it concentrated the majority of the total municipal budget (between \textbf{51\% to 69\%}) onto specific, single barangays for dedicated periods before rotating to the next target.

This ``King of the Hill'' logic is evident in the sequential budget spikes recorded in the financial logs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quarter 2 (The Demologan Siege):} The agent allocated \textbf{68.92\%} of the total LGU budget to Barangay Demologan, raising its compliance to functional stability.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 3 (The Liangan Pivot):} Once Demologan was secured, the agent shifted \textbf{64.52\%} of funds to Barangay Liangan East.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 4 (The Mati Surge):} The focus rotated to Barangay Mati, receiving \textbf{60.64\%} of the quarterly allocation.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 5 (The Ezperanza Shift):} Funds were redirected to Barangay Ezperanza, capturing \textbf{57.20\%} of the budget.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 6 (The Babalaya Operation):} Barangay Babalaya received \textbf{54.08\%} of the allocation.
    \item \textbf{Quarter 7 (The Binuni Targeted):} The agent allocated \textbf{51.15\%} to Barangay Binuni.
    \item \textbf{Quarters 8--12 (The Poblacion Endgame):} Finally, identifying Barangay Poblacion as the most resistant node due to the Urban Resource Trap, the agent committed a sustained \textbf{42.85\%} of the budget for five consecutive quarters to break its inertia.
\end{itemize}

This strategy mirrors the \textit{AI Economist} approach of discovering non-intuitive governance policies that human administrators typically avoid due to perceived unfairness \citep{ZhengAI2022}. The AI learned that by saturating one community until it reached a self-sustaining level of compliance (driven by social norms), it could then safely withdraw funds and move the ``siege'' to the next non-compliant barangay without causing a collapse in previous gains \citep{Constantino2022}. This finding validates the hypothesis of a \textit{Social Tipping Point} \citep{Centola2018, Nyborg2024}, suggesting that effectiveness in resource-constrained environments requires the political will to prioritize sequential efficacy over simultaneous but ineffectual fairness.
